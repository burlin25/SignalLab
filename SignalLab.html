Method & assumptions

Each rate is conversions divided by visitors. Absolute lift is B − A. Relative lift divides that difference by A’s rate and is undefined when A’s rate is zero.

The 95% interval for the difference uses the unpooled normal approximation, Δ ± 1.96 × √[pA(1−pA)/nA + pB(1−pB)/nB]. The rate plots use individual normal intervals clipped to the possible 0–100% range.

The two-sided z-test uses the pooled rate under the null hypothesis of equal rates. The p-value is the probability, under that hypothesis, of a z statistic at least as extreme as the one observed. The decision rules use the difference interval and compare the observed lift with the minimum practical lift.

The analysis assumes independent visitors, random assignment, consistent tracking and a planned stopping point. It does not adjust for repeated peeking or multiple comparisons. Sparse outcomes can make these approximations unreliable.

Estimated annual impact multiplies absolute lift by annual traffic and value per conversion. It assumes the measured lift persists.
